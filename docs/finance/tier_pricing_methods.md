# Tier pricing methods and boundaries

## Scope and data status

The three volume points (90,000, 180,000 and 270,000 units per part) and their material-rate differences are **illustrative project assumptions**. They are not Bharat Forge prices, customer contract terms, supplier quotations or observed project actuals. The raw operational files under `data/` remain simulated and unchanged.

The model now records the selected pricing method with every quote. This matters because the same unit-price ladder can produce different annual revenue depending on the commercial agreement.

## All-units method

Every unit uses the price at the selected tier:

`annual revenue = selected-tier unit price × selected annual volume`

At 180,000 units in the sample, ₹116.51 per unit applies to all units. Annual revenue is ₹20,971,800. The unit cost remains the fully loaded cost calculated at 180,000 units.

## Marginal-band method

Each tier price applies only to units within its incremental band:

`annual revenue = Σ (band units × that band’s unit price)`

At 180,000 units in the same sample, units 1–90,000 use ₹117.33 and units 90,001–180,000 use ₹116.51. Annual revenue is ₹21,045,600, equivalent to an average price of ₹116.92. Unit cost still uses the 180,000-volume cost build. This is a pricing convention, not a claim about a real contract.

## Controls

- The selected volume must be a defined illustrative tier. The engine does not extrapolate a band.
- The first marginal band starts at unit 1. Tier lower bounds are inclusive.
- Each snapshot retains the mode, band allocations, annual revenue, annual cost and contribution.
- Sales approval uses annual revenue excluding GST under the stored method.
- A contract must state whether a discount applies to every unit or only to incremental volume before a customer quote is issued.

## Reconciliation example

| Selection | Annual revenue | Annual cost | Annual contribution |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 180,000, all-units | ₹20,971,800 | ₹18,455,400 | ₹2,516,400 |
| 180,000, marginal bands | ₹21,045,600 | ₹18,455,400 | ₹2,590,200 |

These amounts are generated by `engine/pricing_schedule.py` and asserted in its unit tests. The project’s Excel workbooks retain their prior all-units calculation basis; they are not silently restated as marginal schedules.
