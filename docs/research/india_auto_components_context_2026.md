# India auto-components sector — research note

**Information cut-off:** 18 November 2026.  
**Purpose:** contextualise a hypothetical Pune component manufacturer. This is not a market-sizing exercise for the case company and does not prove an industry-wide RFQ turnaround problem.

## 1. Sector baseline: ACMA FY2025

ACMA's FY2025/FY2024 industry review reports FY25 auto-component turnover of **₹6.73557 lakh crore**, up **9.6% year-on-year**. Supplies to OEMs were **₹5.70145 lakh crore**, exports were **₹1.92326 lakh crore**, and aftermarket turnover was **₹0.99498 lakh crore**. ACMA's presentation reports the sector's FY20–FY25 turnover CAGR as 14%.

**Interpretation:** the sector is economically significant, with OEM supply a large component of the reported industry turnover. This gives context to RFQ commercial operations, but cannot be used as the addressable market or revenue forecast for the hypothetical firm.

## 2. Forward operating context: ICRA, 28 September 2026

ICRA's FY2027 outlook expects domestic OEM supplies to grow **8–10%** and aftermarket revenues **9–11%**. It identifies commodity prices, energy, logistics, currency volatility and geopolitical uncertainty as margin monitorables. The report indicates an FY27 operating-margin outlook range of **11.0–11.5%** for the rated auto-component sector.

**Interpretation:** a faster quote is only commercially valuable if the order has positive incremental contribution after variable costs and the plant can fulfil it. Cost and foreign-exchange changes can move the margin even when volume improves.

## 3. Primary filing examples and comparability discipline

| Company | Period | Disclosed item | Why it is useful | Why it is not a direct benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Samvardhana Motherson International Ltd | FY2024–25 | Revenue from operations ₹1,136,626 million; EBITDA ₹108,775 million; company states revenue included ₹85,706 million from acquisitions during the year | Shows how a primary filing discloses revenue, EBITDA, acquisition contribution and segment composition | Very large diversified group; acquired perimeter and consolidation matter; different product and customer mix |
| Bharat Forge Ltd | FY2024–25 | Consolidated total income ₹153,365.67 million; profit for the year ₹9,132.75 million | Shows the importance of standalone vs consolidated and income vs profit presentation | Total income is not the same definition as revenue from operations; different industry segments and manufacturing mix |

No peer mean, trading multiple, target margin or valuation conclusion is asserted. A defensible trading-comp set would need a common valuation date, consistent consolidated financial definitions, segment fit, net-debt treatment, exceptional-item normalisation and share-price evidence.

## 4. Implications for MarginLadder

- Do not infer the nine-day quote cycle from sector data; reconstruct individual RFQ events.
- Do not equate recovered revenue with contribution or cash.
- Stress-test material, conversion and freight costs where the cost stack supports it.
- Confirm plant capacity and customer delivery requirements before counting recovered orders.
- Treat the 12% case contribution margin as a brief input, not an externally validated sector margin.
