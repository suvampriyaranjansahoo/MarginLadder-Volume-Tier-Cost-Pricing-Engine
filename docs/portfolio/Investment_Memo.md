# MarginLadder — Product Finance investment memo

**Decision:** Should a hypothetical Pune auto-components manufacturer fund broad RFQ workflow and costing automation?

**Recommendation:** Conditional go for measurement and a reversible workflow pilot; no-go for unconditional full rollout.

## Case economics
Assumptions: 60 RFQs/month; 5% lost RFQs; ₹20 lakh average order value; 12% contribution margin; 25% assumed win-back; ₹2 lakh annual run cost; ₹8 lakh implementation fee.

- Annual RFQs: 720; lost RFQs: 36.
- Theoretical lost-RFQ contribution pool: ₹86.4 lakh.
- Modelled recovered contribution at 25% win-back: ₹21.6 lakh.
- Modelled net contribution after annual run cost: ₹19.6 lakh.
- Operating payback: 4.9 months, before tax and working capital.
- Separate cash model payback: 23.6 months under tax and working-capital assumptions.

The opportunity pool is not a forecast. The 25% win-back rate is an assumption, not observed lift. Estimator hours released are capacity, not cash savings unless spend is actually removed.

## Investment gates
1. Instrument RFQ stage timestamps, approval transitions, revisions and loss reasons.
2. Validate baseline completeness and pricing parity on representative RFQs.
3. Pilot queue visibility and approval workflow with pre-defined comparison.
4. Measure adoption, cycle-time distribution, rework, quote accuracy and incremental conversion.
5. Scale only if downside-case incremental contribution exceeds implementation and run costs and plant capacity supports fulfilment.

## Risks
Loss reasons may be miscoded; intervention may not cause conversion lift; adoption may be slow; plant capacity may constrain fulfilment; working capital may delay cash returns. No production customer, interviews, signed UAT, pilot outcomes or realised benefit are claimed.

**Decision:** Buy evidence first. Release capital in stages.
